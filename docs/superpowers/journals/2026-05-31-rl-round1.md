# RL policy-iteration — Round 1 (vs LookV3)

**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Loop goal:** iterate until a trained net beats LookV3, measuring each net BOTH
as PureGnn and as GnnMcts. Plan: `2026-05-31-rl-policy-iteration-loop.md`.
**Round 0 champion:** Cell6 (`round0_Cell6` in the registry).

## What round 1 did

- **Self-play:** 81 games from Cell6 (5 parallel single-exec workers, sims=160,
  full Catan), → 16,887 training positions, 0 timeouts.
- **Train:** warm-start from Cell6, lr 5e-4, rotate-random, early-stop. Best at
  **epoch 1, val_top1=0.444** (early-stopped after ep2 — the now-familiar
  fast-overfit on a small warm-started corpus). → `rl_train_round1/checkpoint_best.pt`.
- **Arena:** `e10e_async`, 80 games, full Catan, **0 timeouts**. Lineup:
  RLr1_PureGnn / RLr1_GnnMcts / Cell6 / LookV3.

## Arena result (80 games)

| Player | Wins | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| **RLr1_GnnMcts** | 41 | **51.2%** |
| LookV3 | 29 | 36.2% |
| Cell6 (PureGnn) | 7 | 8.8% |
| RLr1_PureGnn | 3 | 3.8% |

## Findings

**1. RLr1_GnnMcts beats LookV3 (51.2% vs 36.2%, +15pp).** The loop's stop
criterion ("either mode beats LookV3 by >5pp") is **met** — a trained RL net,
played with MCTS, beats the heuristic baseline.

**2. But it's NOT a clear improvement over Cell6.** Cell6's GnnMcts won 53.8% vs
LookV3 in Gate 2; RLr1's GnnMcts wins 51.2% here. At n=80 (CI ≈ ±11pp) these are
statistically indistinguishable — **round 1 produced a GnnMcts player on par with
Cell6, not better.** (The 51-game partial read 56.9%; the full 80 settled to
51.2% — the early number was optimistic noise.)

**3. RLr1_PureGnn is still terrible (3.8%).** The raw policy head barely moved
under one round of warm-start training (it overfits at epoch 1). The HARD target
— PureGnn beats LookV3 — is far off; the policy alone is not competitive with
search.

## Promotion decision

**Do NOT promote.** RLr1_GnnMcts (51.2%) is not clearly better than Cell6 (53.8%)
— if anything marginally worse, within noise. Promoting it as the new champion
would risk drifting on noise rather than real improvement. **Champion stays
round0_Cell6.** RLr1's checkpoint is preserved (`rl_train_round1/checkpoint_best.pt`,
and copied to the registry as `round1_rlcand.pt` for the bot collection) but is
not the champion.

This is the arena gate doing its job: it beats LookV3 (goal-adjacent) but doesn't
beat the parent, so we don't promote a lateral move.

## What this tells us about the loop

The single-round warm-start recipe reliably produces a **Cell6-equivalent GnnMcts
player that beats LookV3**, but does NOT improve on Cell6, and does NOT fix
PureGnn. To actually advance:

- **The PureGnn weakness is the real wall.** GnnMcts wins because *search* fixes
  the policy's mistakes; the underlying policy is still ~4% vs LookV3. Improving
  PureGnn needs either much more data, more iterations, or a training change
  (the policy head isn't learning a stronger argmax from these targets).
- **Warm-start overfit caps each round at ~1 epoch of real learning.** More data
  per round (the corpus is still small at 16.9k positions) would let more epochs
  help.

## Decision for round 2

The GnnMcts-beats-LookV3 goal is met, but it's a *tie with Cell6*, not progress.
Two honest paths: (a) declare the practical goal met and stop; (b) continue to
chase real improvement (a GnnMcts clearly > Cell6, and/or any PureGnn progress).
Per the user's "continue until Lookahead beaten" — **met at the GnnMcts level** —
but the more meaningful target (PureGnn, or a clearly-stronger GnnMcts) remains.
Proceeding to round 2 from Cell6 (unchanged champion) with a larger corpus to
test whether more data produces real improvement rather than a lateral move.
