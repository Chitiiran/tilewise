## Checkpoint 3 (04:19 EDT, full 60 games) — THE "COMPOUNDING" WAS NOISE
**PureGnn CRASHED to 1.7%** — below even the Cell6 baseline (3.8%), on the
LARGEST corpus (323 games / 256,939 positions). This dispels the apparent trend:

  PureGnn vs LookV3:  3.8 -> 8.3(ckpt1) -> 11.7(ckpt2) -> **1.7(ckpt3)**

The 3.8->11.7 "climb" was SAMPLING VARIANCE on small (~50-60 game) arenas, NOT
real compounding. ckpt3 with the most data produced the WORST PureGnn. GnnMcts
held ~45% (< Cell6 53.8). val_top1 collapsed after ep1 (0.382 -> 0.356 -> 0.343).

**HONEST CONCLUSION: there is NO monotonic compounding at this scale.** More
exploratory data did not steadily lift PureGnn; the noisier+larger targets
degrade a warm-started h128 net as often as they help. AlphaZero exploration is
CORRECT machinery (proven mechanistically: it teaches trades) but needs orders
of magnitude more scale (millions of games) than feasible here for the policy
to stably absorb it. At h128 + few-hundred-games, PureGnn cannot reach LookV3.

**The deployable answer stands: value-fixed GnnMcts beats LookV3 (53.8%, Gate2).**
Search is the irreducible value; the raw policy is a fast approximation that
caps well below search on this compute budget.

## FINAL CONCLUSION (07:05 EDT) — the 9h compounding bet FAILED, honestly

PureGnn vs LookV3 across all measurements:

| ckpt4 (indep seed) | 323 | 5.0% |

**There is NO compounding.** The values (1.7-11.7%) are noise around a low mean
(~6-7%), all within sampling CI of each other, and ALL crushed by LookV3
(~45-49%). The apparent "3.8->11.7 climb" (ckpt1->2) was variance: ckpt3 (most
data) crashed to 1.7%, and ckpt4 (independent seed, same data) gave 5.0% —
two independent draws at the largest scale, both near the floor. val_top1 was
flat-to-declining across all checkpoints (0.378/0.371/0.382/0.352).

**Why:** AlphaZero exploration is mechanistically CORRECT (the divergence
diagnostic proved it teaches trading: override 84->61%, endturn 57->18%), but
the noisier, larger exploratory targets degrade a warm-started h128 net as
readily as they help. Stably absorbing exploration into the policy needs orders
of magnitude more games (real AZ: millions) than feasible here (~hundreds, CPU-
bound at ~0.8 games/min/worker). At h128 + few-hundred games, PureGnn cannot
reach LookV3.

**THE REAL WIN (whole multi-session effort): value-fixed GnnMcts beats LookV3
53.8% (Gate2) — first GNN player ever to** — unlocked by catching the
value-perspective bug. Search is the irreducible value; the raw policy is a fast
approximation that caps well below search on this compute budget. GnnMcts is the
deployable player.
